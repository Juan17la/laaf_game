04 · Product vision

Nostalgic, but not back in time

What LAAF is for, what makes it look and feel the way it does, and who it is made for.

ObjectiveStyleAudiencePrinciplesVoice

Objective

To make players feel hunted, alone and responsible in a fog-bound town, in a horror game short enough to finish in one evening, with an interface that never pulls their eyes out of the fog.

I

Experience goal

Bring back the dread of PSX/PS2-era survival horror (fog, sound, scarcity, being watched) in three tight chapters of about 10 minutes each, with no filler.

II

Narrative goal

Make the player's mercy matter. Sparing or killing each hunter changes who stands with Alex at the end and which of five endings plays.

III

Interface goal

The player always knows the mission, their weapon and ammo, and their vitals, and nothing else competes for attention. Menus feel like part of the world, a case file, not a settings app.

The problem it answers

Classic horror games are loved for their mood but are often hard to return to: tank controls, tiny unreadable menus, no subtitles or comfort options. Modern remakes fix the controls but often lose the texture. LAAF keeps the texture and modernises everything the player touches.

The style that defines it

Analog horror texture under a modern, drawn interface. The world looks like a PS2 game seen through an old CRT: fog, grain, dither, low-poly shapes with painted textures. The UI over it is current-generation: crisp type, rounded shapes, stroke icons, soft shadows and short, purposeful motion. Then the grain passes over the UI too, so the two feel like one picture.

Influences

The PSX/PS2-era Silent Hill games and Cry of Fear set the mood: fog that hides the threat, radio static, small towns with secrets, and grain as a material. LAAF takes the feeling, not the content. Its town, characters, story, art and interface are its own.

LAAF is

  • A dark folder of drawn, rounded UI, set down over the fog
  • Two typefaces: a printed serif for the world, a narrow sans for the controls
  • Icons and shapes before words; a fact as a chip, not a sentence
  • Grain, dither and scanlines over everything, UI included
  • Honest: empty states over invented data, reasons over dead buttons

LAAF is not

  • Squares of text on a black screen ("retro" as an excuse)
  • A modern HUD covered in meters, minimaps and pop-ups at once
  • Pixel fonts or system fonts
  • Gore as spectacle: executions cut away
  • A dashboard to read between threats

Who it is for

PC players, 18 and over, who love story-driven survival horror and the PSX/PS2 era it comes from, and who want an evening of dread, not a 40-hour grind.

PlatformWindows and Linux desktop. Keyboard and mouse first; gamepad fully supported
Age / rating18+, mature horror and violence
LanguagesEnglish and Spanish (text and subtitles)
Play contextOne sitting, evenings, often in the dark with headphones. Tense; they glance at the HUD between threats
Genre literacyHigh. They know fog, radio static and the cult-town premise, and they reward atmosphere and punish filler
Accessibility needsMotion sensitivity, hearing loss, colour-vision deficiency, preference for toggles over holds

Design principles

1
Glance, don't read.

Every HUD element registers in under a second, with shape and icon before words.

2
Quiet until it matters.

Contextual information enters on its event and leaves afterwards. Fog and silence are part of the design.

3
Nostalgia through texture, modernity through craft.

Keep the grain, fog and analog feel; give the UI itself current-generation clarity, motion and polish.

4
One world.

The menu, HUD, overlays and minigames share one visual language. The pause menu is the main menu's dossier, set down over the game.

Voice

The interface talks like the case file it is: short, in-world, and plain about consequences. The serif carries the world's voice; the sans states the facts.

Brand commitments

  • The name is LAAF. The setting is Hollowmere. The terms are the Mark, The Veil, Lantern Tokens, the Harvester, and Moth (Elena).
  • The Silent Hill / Cry of Fear vibe stays: dread, fog, grain, analog texture.
  • The UI is never only squares and text: icons, figures, and rounded, modern forms that fit the game.
  • Show only what the player needs right now: the mission, the guns, the weapons.
  • No invented marketing, reviews or testimonials.
